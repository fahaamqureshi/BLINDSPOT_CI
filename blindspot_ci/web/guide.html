How BLINDSPOT CI works

The problem

A vision model can look great in a demo and still fail on dark, small or half hidden objects. BLINDSPOT CI tests a model on photos or video that a person labeled and tells you whether the evidence supports shipping it, why, and what to fix first.

The four steps

  1. Pick photos or video a person labeled. A person drew a box around the object. A model never writes the labels.
  2. The model outlines the object. The model under test draws its own box.
  3. Plain code scores it against the human labels. Python checks how much the two boxes overlap. No model grades itself.
  4. The release gate decides. A cautious rule reads the scores and gives one of four decisions.

What the four decisions mean

Do not ship yet (NO_GO)
A kind of photo that must work is below the bar.
Not enough evidence yet (NEEDS_REVIEW)
Nothing clearly fails, but the evidence is thin or unchecked.
Passed every check (GO_CANDIDATE)
Every check passed. A person still decides. This is not a safety approval.
Practice data only (DEMO_ONLY)
The answers came from a script or generated images, so they say nothing about the model.

What we have not tested

  • Practice mode shows the software works. It is not model quality.
  • Most labels come from one person.
  • The photo sets are small, so one result can move a lot.
  • We used everyday objects and public clips, not your own factory photos yet.
  • The model may have seen part of the DAVIS video frames in training. The gate flags this.

Real long video test

We ran the model on a public crowd video of 2,405 frames and followed 16 people through it. The first run had a scoring bug that we found and fixed: the model's boxes came back at half size. The verdict was Do not ship yet. Only 3 of 16 people were followed correctly on at least 90 percent of their frames, and the median person was followed on 82.5 percent of frames. People in crowded or dark parts of the clip were often followed with a weak overlap.

Two caveats. Human boxes include hidden body parts, while the model outlines only what it sees. The camera is not fixed. The clip is non-commercial data and is not stored in this repository.

Glossary

IoU
Overlap between two boxes: the shared area divided by the area they cover together. 1.00 is a perfect match.
Wilson interval
A 95 percent range for the true pass rate. Few photos give a wide range on purpose.
Bar
The pass rate a kind of photo must reach. Ours is 9 out of 10.
Kind of photo
A group such as low light or small objects (called a condition in the code).
Release gate
The cautious rule that turns saved scores into one of the four decisions.
SAM 3.1
A Meta model that outlines objects in photos and video. It is the model we tested.
